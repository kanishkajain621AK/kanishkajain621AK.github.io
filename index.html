The problem

A devotional-goods catalogue is unusually hard to merchandise. A customer doesn't arrive looking for "a bracelet" — they arrive looking for something appropriate to their rashi, their deity, or a ritual they've been told to perform. That cuts across a normal collection structure, and the themes flexible enough to express it came loaded with third-party JavaScript.

The constraints

  • Mobile-first. Most traffic arrives on mid-range Android over mobile data.
  • Non-technical merchandising. The catalogue team needed to re-merchandise without a developer.
  • A tight performance budget. Mobile Lighthouse performance started at 60.
  • 800 SKUs with overlapping attributes — one product belongs in several taxonomies at once.

What I built

I wrote the theme from scratch in Liquid rather than buying one — no page builder, no purchased base. Every section is one I own, which meant no inherited JavaScript to work around and no vendor upgrade path to break.

  • Shop-by-rashi and shop-by-deity merchandising across 40+ collections, so the catalogue can be browsed the way customers actually think about it rather than by product type alone.
  • A custom cart with qualified upsells — suggestions are driven by what's already in the cart rather than a fixed "you may also like" list.
  • Build Your Own Combo — a bundle builder with tiered discounting that reprices as items are added, plus three-tier ritual add-ons that attach at cart and order level.
  • Vedic recommendation calculators that turn an advice-seeking visit into a guided purchase by pointing the customer at a specific product.
  • GoKwik one-click checkout with COD verification.
  • A page-transition and motion layer and a conversion-focused product page template, both built natively rather than pulled from a library.

Performance: 60 to 90+

The largest single contributor was removing third-party apps and rebuilding what they did natively — every app removed took its script with it. Owning the theme end to end is what made the rest of the tuning possible: with no inherited code on the page, everything left is there because I put it there.

Result

A storefront doing ~US$600K/month with mobile performance at 90+, where the catalogue team re-merchandises 40+ collections without engineering involvement.

The problem

Reviews and loyalty were running on subscription apps costing over US$1,000 a month combined. The cost was the visible problem; the bigger one was that each app injected its own scripts into the storefront, and neither could be configured into the rules the business actually wanted.

Why build rather than switch

  • The performance work only holds if nothing re-adds vendor scripts later.
  • The rules are specific to this business, and off-the-shelf apps don't model them.
  • At US$12k a year, the build paid for itself quickly and keeps paying.

What I built

Two separate applications, each with its own backend and its own admin. They aren't one system with two faces — they solve different problems, they're deployed and versioned independently, and a fault in one doesn't take the other down.

  • Reviews platform — review capture with photos and video, a moderation queue, and storefront display, fully customisable rather than locked to a vendor's template.
  • Loyalty and rewards programme — the earning and redemption rules the business wanted, surfaced to customers on the storefront.
  • Both fully manageable by non-technical staff. Every rule, tier and moderation action is exposed in an admin the catalogue and support teams use directly. That was the requirement that shaped both builds — a system only an engineer can change is a system that doesn't get changed.

The problem

Personalised astrology readings are valuable to the customer and unscalable by hand — a human astrologer takes days over one detailed report, and the business needed to sell them from ₹299 with a 24-hour promise. One general-purpose prompt doesn't get there either: a single model asked to cover every service produces output that drifts and doesn't hold to the tradition each service is sold under.

The constraints

  • Per-service accuracy. A career reading and a kundli-matching reading follow different rules and need different handling.
  • Consistency across 120+ pages within a single report.
  • A hard delivery window with no human in the loop.
  • Non-technical control. Astrologers had to be added and tuned without a deploy.

What I built

  • A custom admin dashboard controlling 30+ AI astrologers, each trained for a distinct astrology service, with every control over the roster in one place.
  • An automated report pipeline — a birth-chart order placed on the storefront is computed and generated as a 120+ page personalised report, then delivered to the customer automatically within 24 hours, with nobody touching it in between.
  • 16 report products live on the storefront, from ₹299 — career, love, kundli matching, finance, Sade Sati and a full lifeline report among them.

The problem

Indian COD commerce lives and dies on return-to-origin. Every shipment that comes back costs forward freight, return freight and handling, and the margin on the ones that land has to cover all of it. RTO isn't evenly distributed either — it clusters by pincode and by carrier, and a carrier that's excellent in one district can be unreliable two districts over.

The insight

Most stores pick a courier by cost or by a fixed priority list. The more useful question is who actually delivers successfully to this specific pincode — an empirical question that past shipments already answer, if anyone is reading them.

What I built

  • Geolocation capture on the storefront — the site requests the customer's location and resolves it to a pincode before the carrier decision is made.
  • Per-pincode carrier performance built from historical delivery outcomes, so each area has its own picture of which partners actually perform there.
  • Automatic assignment at order creation, mapping the resolved location to the best-performing courier for it without anyone in operations touching the order.

Result

Return-to-origin fell substantially after rollout. On a COD business this is among the highest-leverage systems in the stack, because every avoided return is freight and handling that never gets spent — margin recovered without selling anything more.
